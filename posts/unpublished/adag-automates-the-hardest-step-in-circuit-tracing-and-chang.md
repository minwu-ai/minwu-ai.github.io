## The Bottleneck That Kept Mechanistic Interpretability Toy-Scale

Every attribution graph published before April 2026 shares the same quiet limitation: circuit tracing has relied on ad-hoc human interpretation of each feature's role via manual inspection of activation examples. That is not a minor inconvenience — it is a structural ceiling. Human annotation takes days per circuit; scaling to thousands of prompts or larger models has been practically infeasible.

Submitted April 8, 2026 by Arora, Wu, Steinhardt, and Schwettmann (Stanford / Transluce), [ADAG: Automatically Describing Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07615) introduces a fully automated end-to-end pipeline for describing attribution graphs. Accepted to COLM 2026, it changes the implied scope of what circuit tracing can do.

## How the Pipeline Works

ADAG comprises four stages: circuit tracing, attribution profile computation, automatic clustering of features into supernodes, and automatic natural-language description of supernodes.

The key innovation is the *attribution profile* — quantifying a feature's functional role via input and output gradient effects. A novel clustering algorithm groups features, and an LLM explainer–simulator setup generates and scores natural-language labels. The paper argues these profiles are more informative than max-activating examples or logit-lens inspection because they expose both what inputs a feature depends on and what outputs it causes.

The labeling loop is a two-model setup: an explainer LM proposes labels; a simulator LM scores them on held-out exemplars. On head-to-head evaluation, LLM descriptions match humans on input attributions and are vastly better on output contributions.

## What ADAG Can Actually Do

The paper validates on two tasks that matter for safety practitioners.

**Known circuit recovery.** ADAG automatically replicates a prior human-led study on the multi-hop state capitals task. A concurrent independent pipeline from Patel et al. ([LLMs Can Annotate Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02632)) reaches a similar result: supernodes are as interpretable as those generated by human annotators, recovering the intermediate-hop supernode in 97 of 100 prompts. Two independent groups converging here is meaningful corroboration.

**Jailbreak anatomy.** ADAG is applied to a harmful medical advice jailbreak against Llama 3.1 8B Instruct. The pipeline traces circuits across prompt variants, clusters neurons into 20 supernodes, and identifies which causally drive jailbreak success by steering each supernode and judging outputs. The result: a steerable, identified feature cluster linked to a real attack vector — found without a human reading a single activation example.

## What It Cannot Yet Guarantee

The paper deserves credit for self-disclosure practitioners should weight heavily. A companion finding on arithmetic circuits is quietly damning: autointerp scores validate well — groups are internally coherent — despite carrying weak mechanistic content for the arithmetic task. This suggests autointerp scores measure internal coherence, not meaningful structure.

That is a significant caveat. A high simulator score does not certify that a label captures the *causal* role of the cluster. Anthropic's own [circuit tracing methodology page](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html) acknowledges that attribution graphs provide hypotheses about mechanisms, not conclusions with certainty. ADAG automates hypothesis generation; it does not resolve this epistemic limitation.

A related concern: automated circuit description pipelines, once mature, could plausibly be used to find steerable clusters for harm as readily as to detect them.

## The Governance Read

| Claim | What ADAG Supports | What It Doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| "We identified the circuit responsible for behavior X" | Automated recovery of known circuits ✓ | Completeness; attention heads remain partially outside scope |
| "We found and can steer the jailbreak cluster" | Causal steering demonstrated ✓ | Generalization across prompt variants or model updates |
| "Our safety argument is grounded in circuit analysis" | Mechanistic evidence at scale ✓ | Autointerp score = faithfulness (it doesn't) |

This is the central governance challenge. Mechanistic interpretability is being explored as one possible source of evidence for future AI auditing — but its usefulness remains contingent on validation, coverage, and scalability. ADAG's automation makes circuit tracing genuinely more tractable for audit-at-scale, closing a gap flagged in the context of [model forensics and evidentiary standards](https://minwu-ai.github.io/model-forensics-why-bad-action-observed-is-not-sufficient-ev/) — but automated hypothesis generation is not the same as verified mechanistic understanding.
